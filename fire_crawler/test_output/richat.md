The **Richat Structure**, or ***Guelb er Richât*** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): قلب الريشات, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Qalb ar-Rīšāt*, Hassaniyya: [\[galb er.riːʃaːt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA) <sup>[ⓘ](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:LL-Q56231_(mey)-Tidjani_Saleh-%DA%AF%D9%84%D8%A8_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%AA.wav>)</sup>), often called the **Eye of Africa**[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-2)<sup>[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-3)</sup> is a prominent circular geological feature at the northwestern edge of the [Taoudeni Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoudeni_Basin), on the [Adrar Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrar_Plateau) of the [Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara). It is located near [Ouadane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouadane) in the [Adrar Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrar_Region) of [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania). In [Hassaniya Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassaniya_Arabic), *rīšāt* means *feathers* and it is also known locally in Arabic as *tagense*, referring to the circular opening of the leather pouch that is used to draw water from local wells.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Richard-Molard1952a-1)

It is an eroded [geological dome](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_(geology)>), 40 kilometres (25 mi) in diameter, caused by a subsurface [igneous intrusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_intrusion) deforming the overlying [sedimentary rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock) layers, causing the rock to be exposed as concentric rings with the oldest layers exposed at the centre of the structure. [Igneous rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_rock) is exposed inside and there are [rhyolites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyolite) and [gabbros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabbro) that have undergone [hydrothermal alteration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrothermal_alteration), and a central [megabreccia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breccia). The structure is also the location of exceptional accumulations of [Acheulean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acheulean) [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) stone tools. It was selected as one of the 100 geological heritage sites identified by the [International Union of Geological Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_of_Geological_Sciences) (IUGS) to be of the highest scientific value.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-IUGS2022a-4)

## Description

![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Richat_structure_cropped.png/250px-Richat_structure_cropped.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

The Richat Structure is a deeply [eroded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion), slightly [elliptical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptical_dome) [dome](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_(geology)>) with a diameter of 40 kilometres (25 mi). The sedimentary rock exposed in this dome ranges in age from Late [Proterozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proterozoic) within the center of the dome to [Ordovician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordovician) [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) around its edges. The [sedimentary rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock) composing this structure [dip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_and_dip) outward at 10–20°. Differential erosion of resistant layers of [quartzite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartzite) has created high-relief circular [cuestas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuesta). Its center consists of a [siliceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_dioxide) [breccia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breccia) covering an area that is at least 30 kilometres (19 mi) in diameter.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Matton2008a-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-MattonOthers2005a-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Wooley2001a-7)

![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/ASTER_Richat.jpg/250px-ASTER_Richat.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

Exposed within the interior of the Richat Structure is a variety of [intrusive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intrusive_rock) and [extrusive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrusive_rock) [igneous rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_rock). They include [rhyolitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyolite) volcanic rocks, [gabbros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabbro), [carbonatites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonatite) and [kimberlites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberlite). The rhyolitic rocks consist of [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) flows and [hydrothermally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrothermal_alteration) altered [tuffaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff) rocks that are part of two distinct eruptive centers, which are interpreted to be the eroded remains of two [maars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maar). According to field mapping, [aeromagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeromagnetic_survey), and [gravimetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravimetry) data, the gabbroic rocks form two concentric [ring dikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_dike). The inner ring dike is about 30 metres (98 ft) in width, 3 kilometres (1.9 mi) from the center of the Richat Structure. The outer ring dike is about 70 metres (230 ft) in width, 8 kilometres (5.0 mi) from the center of the structure.<sup>[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:0-8)</sup> Thirty-two carbonatite dikes and [sills](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sill_(geology)>) have been mapped within the structure. The dikes are generally about 300 metres (980 ft) long and typically 1 to 4 metres (3.3 to 13.1 ft) wide. They consist of massive carbonatites that are mostly devoid of [vesicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicular_texture). The carbonatite rocks have been dated as having cooled between 94 and 104 million years ago. A kimberlitic plug and several sills have been found within the northern part of the structure. The kimberlite plug has been dated to around 99 million years old. These intrusive igneous rocks are interpreted as indicating the presence of a large alkaline igneous intrusion that currently underlies the structure and was created by uplifting the overlying rock.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Matton2008a-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-MattonOthers2005a-6)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-NettoOthers1992a-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-MattonOthers2014a-10)

![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/GuelbRichatTopo.png/250px-GuelbRichatTopo.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Guelb_er_Richat_en.jpg/250px-Guelb_er_Richat_en.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

Spectacular hydrothermal features are a part of the Richat Structure. They include the extensive [hydrothermal alteration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrothermal_alteration) of rhyolites and gabbros and a central [megabreccia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breccia) created by hydrothermal dissolution and collapse. The siliceous megabreccia is at least 40 metres (130 ft) thick in its center to only a few meters thick along its edges. The breccia consists of fragments of white to dark gray [cherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chert) material, [quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz)-rich sandstone, diagenetic cherty nodules, and [stromatolitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stromatolite) [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) and is intensively silicified. The hydrothermal alteration, which created this breccia, has been dated to have occurred about 98.2 ± 2.6 million years ago using the [<sup>40</sup>Ar/<sup>39</sup>Ar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argon–argon_dating) method.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Matton2008a-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-MattonOthers2005a-6)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-MattonOthers2014a-10)

## Interpretation

The structure was first described in the 1930s to 1940s, as *Richât Crater* or *Richât buttonhole* (*boutonnière du Richât*). Richard-Molard (1948) considered it to be the result of a [laccolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laccolith) uplift.<sup>[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-11)</sup> A geological expedition to Mauritania led by [Théodore Monod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Théodore_Monod) in 1952 recorded four "crateriform or circular irregularities" (*accidents cratériformes ou circulaires*) in the area, *Er Richât*, *[Aouelloul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aouelloul_crater)* (south of [Chinguetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinguetti)), *[Temimichat-Ghallaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temimichat_Crater)* and *[Tenoumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenoumer_crater)*.<sup>[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-12)</sup> It was initially considered to be an [impact structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_structure) (as is clearly the case with the other three), but a closer study in the 1950s to 1960s suggested that it might instead have been formed by terrestrial processes. After field and laboratory studies in the 1960s, no significant evidence was found for [shock metamorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_metamorphism) or other deformation indicative of a [hypervelocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypervelocity) extraterrestrial impact.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-DietzOthers1969a-13)[Coesite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coesite), an indicator of shock metamorphism, was initially reported as being present in rock samples from the structure, but a further analysis in 1969 concluded that [barite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barite) had been misidentified as coesite.<sup>[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Fudali1969a-14)</sup> Work on dating the structure was done in the 1990s.<sup>[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-15)</sup> A study of the formation of the structure by Matton, et al. (2005, 2008) concluded it was not an impact structure.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Matton2008a-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-MattonOthers2005a-6)

Further analysis of deep structure underneath the surface, including with aeromagnetic and gravimetric mapping,<sup>[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:0-8)</sup> concluded that the structure is the result of ring faults which led to [gabbroic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabbro) [ring dikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_dike) over a large intrusive body of [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma), and the uplifting and later erosion of a dome, through intense [hydrothermal activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrothermal_circulation) through the fractured substructure. This can form [cuestas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuesta) over time through the differential erosion of the resulting alternating hard and soft rock layers.<sup>[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:0-8)</sup> The underlying alkaline igneous complex exposed through erosion dates to the Cretaceous period.[\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-16)

### IUGS geological heritage site

In respect of it being "*a spectacular example of a magmatic concentric alkaline complex*", the [International Union of Geological Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_of_Geological_Sciences) (IUGS) included the Richat Structure in its assemblage of 100 geological heritage sites around the world, in October 2022. The organisation defines an IUGS Geological Heritage Site as "a key place with geological elements and/or processes of international scientific relevance, used as a reference, and/or with a substantial contribution to the development of geological sciences through history."[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-IUGS2022a-4)

## Archaeology

![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Richat_Structure_-_SRTM.jpg/250px-Richat_Structure_-_SRTM.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

• Brown: bedrock

• Yellow/white: sand

• Green: vegetation

• Blue: salty sediments

The Richat Structure is the location of exceptional accumulations of [Acheulean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acheulean) [artifacts](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artifact_(archaeology)>).[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Monod1975a-17)<sup>[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-SaoOthers2008a-18)</sup> These Acheulean archaeological sites are located along [wadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi) that occupy the outermost annular depression of this structure. Pre-Acheulean stone tools also have been found in the same areas. These sites are associated with rubbly [outcrops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outcrop) of [quartzite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartzite) that provided the raw material needed for the manufacture of these artifacts. The most important Acheulean sites and their associated outcrops are found along the northwest of the outer ring, from which Wadi Akerdil heads east and Wadi Bamouere to the west. Sparse and widely scattered [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) spear points and other artifacts have also been found. However, since these sites were first discovered by Théodore Monod in 1974,<sup>[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-Monod1975a-17)</sup> mapping of artifacts within the area of the structure have found them to be generally absent in its innermost depressions. The local apparent wealth of surface artifacts is the result of the concentration and mixing by deflation over multiple [glacial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial)-[interglacial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interglacial) cycles.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-SaoOthers2008a-18)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-GiresseOthers2012a-19)

Artifacts are found, typically redeposited, deflated, or both, in [Late Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene) to early [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) gravelly [mud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud), muddy [gravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel), [clayey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay) [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand), and [silty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silt) sand. These [sediments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment) are often cemented into either [concretionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concretion) masses or beds by [calcrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcrete). Ridges typically consist of deeply weathered [bedrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrock) representing truncated [Cenozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenozoic) [paleosols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleosol) that formed under tropical environments. The Pleistocene to Middle Holocene sediments occur along wadis as the thin, meter- to less-than-meter-thick accumulations in the interior annular depressions to 3-to-4-metre-thick (9.8 to 13.1 ft) accumulations along the wadis in the outermost annular depression of the structure. The gravelly deposits consist of a mixture of slope scree, [debris flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debris_flow), and [fluviatile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluvial) or even torrential flow deposits. The finer-grained, sandy deposits consist of [eolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolian_dust) and [playa lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_lake) deposits. The latter contain well-preserved [freshwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater) [fossils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil). Numerous concordant [radiocarbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiocarbon) dates indicate that the bulk of these sediments accumulated between 15,000 and 8,000 [BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Present) during the [African humid period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_humid_period). These deposits lie directly upon deeply eroded and weathered bedrock.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-GiresseOthers2012a-19)

In addition to Acheulean artifacts, the Richat Structure contains [Middle Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Stone_Age) [Aterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aterian) stone artifacts produced by modern humans, dating to the latest [Middle Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Pleistocene) to [Late Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene) period, around 145,000-29,000 years ago.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-20)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-21)

The protruding land dikes of the Richat Structure are lined with thousands of [stone burial mounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumulus) in various styles.<sup>[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-22)</sup> These still remain to be excavated in order to establish their age. Several rock art sites have also been identified, which include depictions of horsemen armed with [javelins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javelin), [chariots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariot), bovids, elephants (Tililit, Oued Slil) and [Libyco-Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyco-Berber_alphabet) inscriptions (Tin Labbé, Lemqader).[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-23)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-24)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-25)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-26)

## Fringe theory of Atlantis site

The Richat Structure has been the subject of unsubstantiated [fringe claims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fringe_theory) to be the site of [Atlantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis) mentioned in the works of [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato).[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:1-27)<sup>[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-28)</sup> This claim is primarily based on the [concentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentric_objects) nature of the structure, which superficially matches Plato's description of the city.<sup>[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:1-27)</sup> Most classicists believe that Atlantis was a fictional [rhetorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhetoric) invention by Plato, rather than a real geographic location.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-29)<sup>[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-30)</sup> According to archaeologist Sean M. Rafferty, other than superficially matching Plato's Atlantis description in being circular, in its particular details, including being a geologically ancient natural structure, the Richat Structure bears little resemblance to Plato's description of Atlantis, with the Richat Structure's location far inland in a desert contradicting Plato's information regarding the location of Atlantis.<sup>[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-:1-27)</sup> Skeptic [Steven Novella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Novella) criticised other inconsistencies in the claim, including the lack of any archaeological evidence for a city having been built there, and the lack of evidence for the canals mentioned in Plato's Atlantis account.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org#cite_note-31)
